Privacy policy

Effective October 9, 2026

HearthTerm is a desktop SSH and local-shell workspace published by Gosh Apps. This policy covers the application, its optional Google Sync feature, and this website. For privacy or support questions, contact linouser@gmail.com.

HearthTerm is in pre-release development. The Google Sync implementation described below is being prepared and validated for public release; it is not yet advertised as a publicly available, Google-verified service.

You choose where your vault lives. Local-only vaults need no account. If you enable Google Sync, HearthTerm stores your selected vaults in your Google account, including saved secrets and the key needed to recover them. Google account access can therefore recover those secrets.

Data on your computer

HearthTerm stores the information you save: host addresses, usernames, passwords, private SSH keys and passphrases, identities, proxy settings, groups, tags, snippets, tunnel definitions, trusted host keys, shell profiles, preferences, and saved terminal layouts. Vault records are encrypted on your computer. A local master password protects the vault's data key; optional quick unlock uses your operating system's credential store.

Terminal output and scrollback stay in process memory and are not saved in the vault or uploaded by Google Sync. Running processes, live connections and tunnels, external files, and keys held only by an external SSH agent are not copied between computers. Device-specific settings, such as discovered shells, window geometry, and agent socket paths, stay on that device.

The app keeps local diagnostic logs and non-secret configuration. Logs do not contain saved passwords, private keys, or terminal contents. HearthTerm has no telemetry or automatic diagnostic upload, and Gosh Apps operates no backend that receives your vaults.

Optional Google account access

Google Sync begins only when you choose to connect Google and authorize access in your browser. You choose which existing local vaults to enroll. The app requests these permissions:

  • openid and email: identify your Google account, associate it with its vaults, and show the connected account. The app receives your account identifier and email address, and may receive a display name in Google's identity response.
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdata: read and write HearthTerm's files in Google Drive's hidden application-data area for synchronization and recovery. This does not grant access to your ordinary Drive files, Gmail messages, contacts, or calendars.

The app sends enrolled vault records directly from your computer to Google over HTTPS and downloads them to your authorized computers. This includes saved passwords, private keys, passphrases, portable preferences, and per-computer layouts. Google stores encrypted change history, previous versions, conflict alternatives, recovery information, and sync metadata such as vault names and account, device, and file identifiers. HearthTerm uses this information to restore your vaults, apply changes, and let you resolve conflicts.

Google authorization credentials and device unlock keys are remembered in the OS credential store when available. Without a usable credential store, sign-in lasts for the app session; the app does not write a plaintext token fallback. Local account and sync metadata can include your email, account and device identifiers, vault names, file identifiers, and sync cursors.

Recovery trusts your Google account. Encrypted vault history and its recovery key are stored in the same account's application data. This is not end-to-end encryption against Google or someone who gains access to that account or its authorized app data. A local vault password does not prevent Google recovery and does not remove that access. Choose a local-only vault if you do not want account recovery.

Google handles requests and stored data under Google's Privacy Policy. Google account availability, authorization, storage limits, and service policies can affect sync and recovery.

How data is used and shared

HearthTerm uses information received from Google APIs only for the account identification, synchronization, and recovery features described here. These limits also apply to transfers of that information. Google's requirements for API data are described in the Google API Services User Data Policy and the Google Workspace API User Data and Developer Policy, including their Limited Use requirements.

Gosh Apps does not sell Google user data, use it for advertising, or use it to train AI models. The application does not send your Google tokens or vault contents to Gosh Apps for human review. Your authorized computers and Google process the data required for sync; there is no HearthTerm cloud account or separate developer-hosted vault store.

SSH connections, proxy connections, and tunnels send the traffic and authentication material needed for the destinations you choose. Programs you run inside a terminal can access files or make their own network requests under your operating system permissions. Those programs and remote services have their own data practices.

Retention and deletion

Local vaults remain on your computer until you remove their files. Google Sync retains encrypted history without automatic pruning, including older versions of secrets you later change or delete. The app's removal options have different effects:

  • Disconnect Google removes this installation's remembered Google credentials and device recovery keys, locks the vault, and stops its Google access. Local vault files and cloud history remain. Other computers are not disconnected.
  • Remove from this computer hides a vault from that installation and clears its device unlock key. The encrypted local database and Google history remain.
  • Delete everywhere removes a vault from the synced vault list when connected computers next sync. It leaves encrypted history and recovery information in Google Drive and can leave local copies. It is not secure erasure.

To delete retained cloud data, disconnect HearthTerm on your computers, then open Google Drive settings, choose Manage apps, find HearthTerm, and delete its hidden app data. This removes the application's stored cloud data for all its vaults in that Google account. See Google's hidden-app-data instructions. Google controls deletion and retention within its own systems.

You can also revoke HearthTerm's authorization in your Google Account connections. Revoking permission does not itself erase saved cloud history or local files.

Remove local encrypted databases and external backups separately using your operating system. Uninstalling the application may leave its data directory behind. Default data locations are:

  • Linux: ~/.local/share/hearthterm, or $XDG_DATA_HOME/hearthterm if that environment variable is set. Flatpak uses ~/.var/app/com.goshapps.HearthTerm/data/hearthterm.
  • macOS: ~/Library/Application Support/com.goshapps.HearthTerm.
  • Windows: %APPDATA%\goshapps\HearthTerm\data.

If you set HEARTHTERM_DATA_DIR, use that directory instead. Gosh Apps cannot delete files on your devices or recover a forgotten local-only vault password.

This website and support

This is a static website hosted on GitHub Pages. It does not use analytics scripts, tracking cookies, advertising, or externally loaded fonts. GitHub logs visitors' IP addresses for security purposes; see GitHub Pages data collection and the GitHub Privacy Statement. Following a link to Google or GitHub subjects that visit to the destination's policies.

If you email support, the recipient and email providers receive your email address and the message you choose to send. Gosh Apps uses that correspondence to respond and handle the reported issue. Do not send vault databases, private keys, passwords, recovery keys, or terminal contents. Contact linouser@gmail.com with privacy questions or requests concerning support correspondence.

Security and policy updates

Encryption does not protect an unlocked vault from malware or someone who controls your computer. Account recovery also depends on the security of your Google account. Report suspected vulnerabilities privately to linouser@gmail.com with the app version, platform, and a description. Do not include vault files, secrets, or terminal output.

Changes to this policy will be published on this page with an updated effective date. The policy describes HearthTerm's current features; publishing it does not mean Google has verified or approved the app.
